\begin{abstract}
This working investigation asks how normalization changes the learning of rare
binary features under a finite training budget. For a fixed block of common
predictors and one rare binary feature, we derive a population gradient-flow
limit in which incomplete common-feature learning contributes to the rare
coefficient even as common--rare Pearson correlations vanish. Exact finite-update
formulas for gradient descent and fresh-sample stochastic gradient descent
separate initial uncertainty, multiplicative update variance, and observation
noise. Under a bounded common-block assumption, an exact second-moment operator
criterion yields stability thresholds across small, intermediate, and large
products of batch size and rare prevalence. The results use known population
centering and fixed scales. General finite-sample guarantees,
comparisons with adaptive optimizers, and empirical usefulness remain research
objectives. Earlier weighted-lasso and ridge results are retained as
explicit-regularization baselines; no standalone novelty claim is made for
these calculations.
\end{abstract}
