\begin{abstract}
This working investigation asks how normalization changes the learning of rare
binary features under a finite training budget. Two elementary calculations
motivate the research program. In standardized population gradient flow, a rare
coefficient can retain a transient contribution from a common feature even as
Pearson correlation vanishes. In scalar stochastic gradient descent, unit
population curvature coexists with a step-size restriction governed by feature
rarity and batch size. These calculations separate average optimization
geometry from the higher moments of stochastic updates. The proposed study
will connect dependence, normalization, and sampling to learning trajectories
and prediction within rare subgroups. General finite-sample guarantees,
comparisons with adaptive optimizers, and empirical usefulness remain research
objectives. Earlier weighted-lasso and ridge results are retained as
explicit-regularization baselines; no standalone novelty claim is made for
the elementary calculations.
\end{abstract}
