\begin{abstract}
  Feature normalization changes the relative rates at which stochastic gradient
  descent learns different coordinates. We study its consequences for prediction
  under finite training budgets, using common and rare binary features as a
  controlled setting. Exact error moments separate incomplete learning,
  randomness in feature sampling, and observation noise. With a declared
  common-coordinate rate calibration, we certify parameter regions where
  standard-deviation scaling reverses its rare-subgroup risk ordering as
  training continues, and where aggregate and rare-subgroup risk favor opposite
  scaling rules. These are comparisons of specified training procedures: both
  original anchor comparisons disappear under a subsequent learning-rate and
  stopping search. Experiments with estimated preprocessing and validation
  selection retain differences in relative learning. On a sparse categorical
  task, expanding the rate search removes a resolved standardization advantage
  under aggregate selection, while a damped scaling retains lower risks.
  Selection also exposes severe failures when rare cases are absent from
  validation. Together, the results describe how normalization interacts with
  the training budget and prediction objective, while delimiting what
  fixed-configuration comparisons establish about a tuned procedure.
\end{abstract}
