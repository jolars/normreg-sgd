\subsection{Finite-prevalence comparisons with the limiting risks}
\label{sec:oracle-finite}

We compare \cref{thm:oracle-limits,cor:oracle-allocation} with exact moments
under the same population model. The saved study contains 43 signal, noise, and
target-budget settings at six prevalences from $0.04$ to $2 \times 10^{-6}$. The
settings include both finite-prevalence theorem anchors at $\tau = 1,2,4$;
common signals at 90\%, 100\%, and 110\% of the predicted crossing; eight
allocations with $s/4 + r = 1$ and $\tau = 2$; four noise levels; points around
U's rare-learning threshold; budgets from $0.01$ to $1000$; and zero-signal and
zero-noise endpoints. These are selected slices rather than uniform coverage of
the surrounding parameter ranges. \Cref{fig:oracle-finite} summarizes the
comparisons.

Each scaling minimizes expected rare risk over separate finite grids. The fine
rate grid contains 2049 fractions of its mean-square stability limit, combining
1025 linear and 1025 logarithmic points, with zero and positive fractions from
$10^{-10}$ to $1 - 10^{-6}$. Limiting optimal rates are not inserted into the
grid, and rates outside the stable interval are not searched. Stopping grids
contain initialization, every integer through 64, all caps, and rounded
geometric checkpoints up to the largest cap. They have 663 to 883 distinct
stops, depending on prevalence. The largest cap is $500{,}001{,}001$. Matrix
powers of the affine recurrence evaluate every candidate without Monte Carlo
noise.

Caps round $\tau/b$ upward, except at target $\tau = 4$, where they round
downward to stay within the finite-prevalence theorem's range. Primary errors
use $L_A(Mb)$, and the saved results also retain predictions at the nominal
target. At $q = 0.04$, target $\tau = 0.01$ gives one observation and actual
$Mb = 0.0384$, so the coarse-budget discrepancy includes appreciable rounding.

Coarse grids take every second generating rate and geometric stop, retaining the
early integer stops and all caps. Separate rate refinement, stop refinement, and
joint refinement assess search resolution. The largest decrease under joint
refinement is $1.362 \times 10^{-5}$. Stop refinement alone changes no selected
risk: every positive-stop selection uses its cap, and the others use
initialization. No positive-stop selection reaches a rate-grid edge. These
observations do not bound the error relative to the unrestricted oracle or prove
that full-cap training is always optimal at finite prevalence.

Both theorem orderings hold in every sampled anchor comparison. At
$q = 2 \times 10^{-6}$, the largest anchor errors are $2.987 \times 10^{-6}$ for
U and $1.536 \times 10^{-6}$ for S. Across all 43 settings, the corresponding
maxima are $2.01631 \times 10^{-4}$ and $2.27583 \times 10^{-6}$; the largest U
error occurs at target $\tau = 0.01$. Every setting with a nonzero nominal
limiting gap has the predicted sign at the smallest prevalence. The sampled
common-signal crossings are bracketed by 90\% and 110\% of their limiting
values, and the allocation slice recovers the ordering on both sides of
$\theta_* \approx 0.83770278$. Cases exactly at limiting ties remain sensitive
to rounding and search resolution and do not locate a finite-prevalence
boundary.

The U endpoint has a concrete finite-prevalence interpretation. At
$\tau = 2,s = 1,r = 0.01,\nu = 1$, the limiting rate is zero and
$L_U = r/2 = 0.005$. The selected finite rate nevertheless remains positive,
falling from $0.07003$ at $q = 0.02$ to $2.866 \times 10^{-5}$ at
$q = 2 \times 10^{-6}$, while the risk falls from $0.01570$ to $0.00500127$.
These rates learn the common signal; literal zero-rate training would retain its
error.

\paragraph{A finite-prevalence disagreement.}

For $s = 0.1,r = 1$, target $\tau = 2$, and $\nu = 100$, the limiting risks
favor U: $L_U = 0.500000$ and $L_S \approx 0.502306$. The finite-grid SD-minus-U
gaps are instead $-0.00568756$ at $q = 0.02$, $-0.00563282$ at $q = 0.002$, and
$-0.000608220$ at $q = 0.0002$. They become positive at $q = 0.00002$ and
$q = 0.000002$, with gaps $0.00180198$ and $0.00223702$. These discrepancies
exceed the observed refinement changes, showing that the limiting decision can
be inaccurate at finite prevalence. They do not certify a reversal of the
unrestricted finite-prevalence oracles.

At $s = r = \nu = 1$ and the smallest prevalence, U wins at target $\tau = 1$,
while S wins at $\tau = 10,100,1000$. At $\tau = 1000$, the finite risk ratio is
$0.839843$ and the limiting ratio is $0.839507$, both still above $4/5$. These
comparisons preserve the distinction between the prevalence limit and the
subsequent large-budget limit.

\paragraph{Reproduction and independent replay.}

The settings, grids, all four searches, selected risks, rates, stops, and
summaries are saved under \path{results/oracle-finite/}. The checker
\path{checks/oracle_finite.py} independently constructs observation-level tensor
updates and tests all four nested searches on a small problem. It also replays
all 2064 saved selections with full tensor updates and long-double matrix
powers; the recorded maximum discrepancy is $7.38 \times 10^{-11}$. Run the
following commands to regenerate the searches and summaries, reconstruct every
selection, and plot the saved results, respectively:
\begin{verbatim}
task experiment:oracle-finite
task check:oracle-finite -- --rerun
task figure:oracle-finite
\end{verbatim}
Without \texttt{--rerun}, the checker replays saved selections without repeating
the full searches. Summarizing, plotting, and building the paper never launch
these experiments.

\Cref{fig:intro-signal-crossing} uses the allocation path in
\cref{cor:oracle-allocation}: $s = 4(1 - \theta)$ and $r = \theta$, with
$\nu = 1$ and $\tau = 2$. Thus $s/4 + r = 1$ and initial limiting subgroup risk
stays at $1/2$. The middle tick, $\theta = 1/2$, gives equal common and rare
contributions to this risk. We plot the scalar minima in
\cref{eq:limiting-objectives}, saved as the nominal limiting risks of the
allocation cases in \path{results/oracle-finite/summary.json}. The eight
allocation values are $0$, $1/4$, $1/2$, $3/4$, $1$, and $\theta_* - .02$,
$\theta_*$, $\theta_* + .02$, where scalar root finding gives
$\theta_* \approx .8377$. Lines connect these saved values;
\cref{cor:oracle-allocation} supplies the unique-crossing guarantee. Unlike the
finite-prevalence curves in \cref{fig:oracle-finite}(b), this panel uses the
limiting risks at exactly $\tau = 2$, without integer-cap rounding.

Run \texttt{task} \path{check:oracle-limits} to check the scalar solver and
saved comparisons, and \texttt{task} \path{figure:intro} to plot the saved
values without new optimization or training. The earlier study at fixed
unweighted squared coefficient sum remains in \path{results/signal-allocation/}
