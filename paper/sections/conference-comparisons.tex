\section{Two normalization-risk comparisons}
\label{sec:conference-comparisons}

\subsection{Learning the rare signal versus fitting noise}

Under independence, SD scaling changes the rare mean contraction from $1 - 4hb$
to $1 - h$. Its per-update rare-coordinate noise injection changes from
$16h^2b\sigma^2/B$ to $h^2\sigma^2/(bB)$. It therefore accelerates learning
while increasing the injection of observation noise. Random feature sampling
contributes additional variance to both procedures.

The diagonal moments form a nonnegative two-dimensional recurrence. Dropping its
off-diagonal coupling yields a scalar approximation to rare risk with an
explicit nonnegative remainder. If $F$ and $N$ denote the noiseless and
unit-noise parts of the SD-minus-U risk contrast, the approximation gives bounds
$F_- \leq F \leq F_+$ and $N_- \leq N \leq N_+$. When $F_+ < 0 < N_-$, the
noise-variance threshold $\tau = -F/N$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{-F_+}{N_+} \leq \tau
                  \leq \frac{-F_-}{N_-}. \label{eq:conference-threshold}
\end{equation}
The remainder accounts for coupling through sampled updates even when the
population coordinates are independent. Its derivation and all quantities are
given in Appendix~\ref{sec:comparison-proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Stopping and batch reversal]
 \label{thm:conference-c1}
 In the binary model with $\ell = 0$, suppose
 \begin{gather*}
  q \in[0.019,0.021] ,\quad h \in[0.0019,0.0021] ,               \\
  \beta_C^*,\beta_R^* \in[0.95,1.05] ,\quad\sigma^2 \in[1.5,5] .
 \end{gather*}
 Both U and S are mean-square stable for $B \in \{1,10\}$. Their rare-risk
 contrast $\Delta_{B,k} = R_{R,k}^S - R_{R,k}^U$ lies in the intervals in
 Table~\ref{tab:conference-c1} throughout this parameter box.
\end{theorem}

\begin{table}[t]
 \centering
 \begin{tabular}{rrrr}
  \toprule
  $B$ &    $k$ & $M = kB$ &    $\Delta_{B,k}$ \\
  \midrule
    1 &  1,000 &    1,000 &   $[-.353,-.160]$ \\
    1 & 10,000 &   10,000 &    $[.0021,.125]$ \\
   10 &  1,000 &   10,000 &   $[-.386,-.286]$ \\
   10 & 10,000 &  100,000 & $[-.0281,-.0016]$ \\
  \bottomrule
 \end{tabular}
 \caption{
  Uniform rare-risk gap bounds for Theorem~\ref{thm:conference-c1}. Negative

 }

\end{table}

At $B = 1$, the normalization ordering reverses as training continues. At
$M = 10{,}000$, it also differs between $B = 1$ and $B = 10$. This compares
normalization rules within each budget configuration; it does not order batch
sizes within either rule.

For the anchor $q = .02$, $h = .002$, $\beta^* = (1,1)^\top$, and
$\sigma^2 = 3$, the early $B = 1$ rare risk is $.356678$ for U and $.090407$ for
S. The squared-bias contributions are $.353207$ and $.011040$, respectively. At
$k = 10{,}000$, U has learned enough of the rare signal to attain risk
$.028145$, whereas S has risk $.078788$, almost entirely from observation noise.
The exact calculation includes feature-sampling variance throughout.

\subsection{Aggregate and rare-presence objectives}

Dependence can change the ordering across prediction objectives. Let
$\Delta V = V_k^S - V_k^U$. Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:conference-risks}
gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_R - \Delta_A = \frac{1 - 2q}{2}\bigl\{(1 - q)\Delta V_{RR} + \ell\Delta V_{CR}\bigr\}, \label{eq:conference-objectives}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_A$ and $\Delta_R$ denote aggregate and rare-risk gaps. The cross
moment includes both the product of coefficient biases and their centered
covariance.

\begin{theorem}[Objective disagreement]
 \label{thm:conference-c2}
 Take $q = .02$, $\ell \in[.9,1]$, $\beta^* = (2,1)^\top$, $h = .01$, $B = 1$,
 $k = M = 100$, and $\sigma^2 \in[3.8,4.1]$. Both U and S are mean-square
 stable, and uniformly over these intervals,
 \begin{align*}
  .00077 & \leq R_k^S - R_k^U \leq .00194,         \\
  -.0983 & \leq R_{R,k}^S - R_{R,k}^U \leq -.0233.
 \end{align*}
 Moreover, $V_{CC,k}^S > V_{CC,k}^U$ and $V_{RR,k}^S > V_{RR,k}^U$.
\end{theorem}

SD scaling increases each coefficient's mean squared error but improves
rare-presence prediction. At $\ell = 1$ and $\sigma^2 = 4$,
\[
 \Delta V \approx \begin{pmatrix}
                   .022743  & -.615146 \\
                   -.615146 & .470335
                  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The much smaller joint error product offsets the larger individual errors for
rare predictions. The aggregate risk assigns the cross moment weight $.01$,
whereas rare risk assigns weight $.49$. This explains why separate coefficient
error rankings can miss the prediction benefit.

Both theorems use outward-rounded rational interval certificates over continuous
parameter boxes. Appendix~\ref{sec:comparison-proofs} supplies the recurrences
and enclosure arguments. The results establish finite-budget comparisons for
stated procedures. They do not compare continuously optimized rates, all
stopping rules, or optimal normalization, and the target-risk identity itself is
elementary.
