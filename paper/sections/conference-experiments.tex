\section{From calibrated comparisons to selected procedures}
\label{sec:conference-experiments}

The experiments ask which effects remain when we change the comparison protocol.
We distinguish three settings: exact fresh-sample moments, replacement SGD on
sampled noisy training sets, and held-out evaluation on a fixed categorical
dataset. An observation budget counts every sampled training row, including
repeated visits. All results below concern final iterates selected from stored
checkpoints.

\subsection{Rate and stopping adjustments}

A follow-up exact-moment study covers 228 binary laws, 51,048 training
configurations, and 2,571,800 endpoints. It varies prevalence, dependence,
signal, noise, rates, batches, and stopping, and retains fixed-update and
fixed-observation comparisons. The primary adjusted comparison selects the
lowest exact risk among mean-square stable configurations and initialization. It
uses a coarse rate range from $1.953125 \times 10^{-6}$ to $.64$, with a
separate resolution check on 16 laws. This is oracle selection with a known test
law, not validation selection.

Both original anchor comparisons disappear after the declared adjustments. For
the independent anchor of Theorem~\ref{thm:conference-c1},
Table~\ref{tab:conference-adjustment} reports the rare-risk gap at the fixed
configuration and after rate/stopping selection. The late $B = 1$ disadvantage
for S disappears; the selected rates are $h_U = .004$ and $h_S = .0003125$. At
the dependent anchor of Theorem~\ref{thm:conference-c2}, adjusted selection
favors U under both objectives. Other laws retain reversals or produce objective
disagreement. The theorems therefore describe a calibrated procedure, while the
search answers a different question about the available family of procedures.

The study retains invalid candidates in unrestricted reports rather than
silently removing them. Some unrestricted comparisons remain unresolved because
of overflow or invalid moment calculations. A paired Monte Carlo covariance
diagnostic also exceeds its declared gate in one tail-dominated case; a
conditional-absence investigation is retained with that result. Neither issue is
converted into a claim of complete simulation agreement. The exact stable
comparisons and these diagnostic limits have separate roles in the evidence.

\subsection{Estimated preprocessing and validation selection}

The sampled-data stage uses eight binary laws, 128 independent replications per
law, $n = 256$ training observations, and 128 validation observations. It
compares known preprocessing, estimated scales, training centering with a fixed
or fitted intercept, and an uncentered fitted-intercept arm. The feature
divisors are $1$ for no scaling, $\sqrt{v_j}$ for SD scaling, and
$\sqrt{v_j + 0.01}$ for damped scaling \eqref{eq:conference-damped}. Estimated
variances use denominator $n$. Constant training columns are frozen at zero
coefficient with divisor one. Fitted intercepts start at the training response
mean. Appendix~\ref{sec:practical} gives further protocol details.

Each scaling and preprocessing arm receives the same five rate factors
\[
 \eta = \frac{a_0}{\max(L,10^{-12})},\qquad a_0 \in \{.001,.01,.05,.2,.8\},
\]
where $L$ is the largest empirical curvature, including the intercept coordinate
when fitted. This calibration does not guarantee stochastic stability. Batches
are $B \in \{1,16\}$ and observation caps are $M \in \{128,512,2048\}$. We
compare fixed $a_0 = .05$, $B = 16$ with validation selection of rate and batch,
or rate, batch, and stopping. Initialization is eligible. Ten paths per arm and
scaling cost $10M$ processed observations at cap $M$; checkpoints share paths.
Selection never uses test outcomes, and selected models are not refitted.

For the independent equal-signal law at $q = .02$ and $\sigma^2 = 3$, the
centered, fitted-intercept arm has fixed-configuration rare-risk gaps of
$-.1653$ (paired MCSE $.0295$) at $M = 128$ and $+.0901$ ($.0697$) at
$M = 2048$. The early advantage is clear; the late disadvantage is within two
MCSE of zero. At the latter budget, the rare-only signal favors S by $.2320$
($.0233$), while the common-only signal favors U by $.1272$ ($.0159$). These
sampled-data results use empirical rate calibration, so they do not extend the
exact population theorems. Figure~\ref{fig:conference-practical} shows the fixed
paths and the categorical-task comparisons described below.

\paragraph{When validation contains no rare cases.}

At $q = .002$, the two anchor laws have empty rare validation groups in 106 and
105 of 128 replications. The declared rare-selection rule falls back to
aggregate loss in those cases. One selected centered SD fit has rare coefficient
about $-6.719 \times 10^{16}$ and validation half squared loss $2.086$, but
exact-cell rare loss about $2.257 \times 10^{33}$. A 160-digit decimal replay of
all eligible trajectories in the worst selected replication of each of 24
law/scaling panels leaves all 24 selections unchanged. The failure is not
explained by detected floating-point cancellation. Extreme finite outcomes and
151 failed synthetic endpoints remain in the archive; tail-dominated means are
not precise population-risk estimates.

\subsection{A sparse categorical prediction task}

We predict the recorded poisonous label in UCI Mushroom \citep{uciMushroom}
using squared loss and all 22 categorical predictors. The declared subgroup is
green spore-print color: 72 of 8124 records. Training data alone determine the
one-hot vocabulary, means, and scales. Missingness is a category and unknown
categories map to zero blocks. The design uses sparse storage and algebraic
centering. Coefficients are reported on the original feature scale; in the
rank-deficient full one-hot design, they are optimizer-dependent rather than
uniquely identified effects. Predictions are not clipped.

We use 32 random partitions at each training/validation size $(256,128)$ and
$(2048,1024)$, with remaining records reserved for testing. Candidates share
partitions and replacement index streams. Each scaling receives the same five
rate factors and $B \in \{16,128\}$, with $M \in \{512,2048,8192\}$. In that
frozen study, all 32 large-sample unscaled aggregate selections choose the upper
rate factor $.8$. A separately declared follow-up expands to 17 factors from
$.001$ to $6.4$ for every scaling and arm, retaining the same splits, index
streams, and stopping checkpoints. Each scaling now receives 34 trajectories,
costing $34M$ observations at cap $M$. A further declared comparison adds
variance scaling, $s_j = v_j$, and gives all four rules 25 factors from
$10^{-5}$ to $409.6$, costing $50M$ per rule.
Table~\ref{tab:conference-mushroom} reports this four-rule comparison.
Appendices~\ref{sec:mushroom-rate-search}
and~\ref{sec:variance-scaling-followup} retain paths, failures, nested searches,
and separate stopping-resolution checks.

Rate expansion reduces the unscaled aggregate-selected rare loss from $.012482$
to $.005955$. The SD-minus-none rare gap shrinks from $-.006570$ (paired MCSE
$.001526$) to $-.001192$ ($.001318$), within two MCSE of zero. The original SD
advantage is no longer resolved; this does not establish equivalence. Damping
retains an advantage in both aggregate loss, $-.000532$ ($.000056$), and rare
loss, $-.005273$ ($.000797$). Under rare-objective selection, the SD rare
advantage persists at $-.001421$ ($.000470$), with highly variable aggregate
loss. Its aggregate-loss median is $.004357$ and maximum is $2.36513$.

Variance scaling's rare disadvantage against none is $.045630$ (paired MCSE
$.008323$), whereas its aggregate difference $.310647$ ($.201754$) is unresolved
by the two-MCSE convention. Its aggregate median is $.069802$ and maximum
$6.505207$. The wider grid leaves all original-stop Mushroom selected predictors
unchanged from the nested 17-factor search, while placing every positive-budget
selection away from both rate edges, including in the refined stopping search.
This is a finite-grid conclusion, not continuous optimality.

All 32 unscaled aggregate selections now use interior factors $.6$--$1.8$, with
worse validation scores at their immediate higher tested neighbors. This
resolves the original upper edge within the finite grid, not a continuous
optimum. The expanded mean green coefficients are $.5999$, $.7763$, and $.9126$
for none, SD, and damping; other features partly offset these contributions.
Across both sizes and all arms, the 17-factor study retains 2950 failed
endpoints. Its secondary uncentered rare-objective search selected the upper
edge when extra early stops were allowed; the four-rule extension resolves that
finite-grid boundary. The small-sample experiment has no green training
observations in five partitions and none in validation in thirteen. MCSEs
describe partition and optimization variation within this dataset. The source
comprises hypothetical descriptions derived from a field guide, so random-record
performance does not establish generalization to new species or field data.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the damping offset.}

A separately declared comparison in the centered, fitted-intercept arm tests
$s_j = \sqrt{v_j + \tau}$ at five fixed positive offsets, with equal rate,
batch, and stopping searches per rule. At $n = 2048$, $M = 8192$, and aggregate
validation, the tested offsets $.003$, $.01$, $.03$, and $.1$ retain both risk
improvements over none beyond two paired MCSE; at $.001$, the aggregate
improvement is unresolved. At $n = 256$, rare validation selects an extreme
finite path at $\tau = .03$, so damping does not prevent selection failures.
That study also includes variance scaling; the four-rule extension above
addresses its rate edge. Appendix~\ref{sec:mushroom-sensitivity} gives the
results and their limits.
