\section{Normalization and the training procedure}
\label{sec:conference-setting}

Let $x$ be centered, $C=\E[xx^\top]\succ0$, and
$y=x^\top\beta^*+\varepsilon$, with
$\E[\varepsilon\mid x]=0$ and
$\E[\varepsilon^2\mid x]=\sigma^2$. A diagonal matrix $S$ contains
positive feature divisors. Training on $z=S^{-1}x$ with parameter
$\theta=S\beta$ gives the original-scale SGD update
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{k+1}=\beta_k-\frac{\eta S^{-2}}{B}
 \sum_{i=1}^B x_{k,i}(x_{k,i}^\top\beta_k-y_{k,i}).
 \label{eq:conference-update}
\end{equation}
The loss is averaged and has a factor $1/2$. The population comparisons fix
the centered intercept at zero and initialize $\beta_0=0$. Each update draws
$B$ independent fresh observations, so $k$ updates use $M=kB$ observations.
Section~\ref{sec:conference-experiments} separately studies replacement
sampling from a fixed training set of size $n$; there, $M$ counts processed
observations and can exceed $n$.

Global rescaling has an exact scalar compensation: replacing $S$ by $gS$
and $\eta$ by $g^2\eta$ leaves every update unchanged on a shared data
stream. Changing relative feature scales generally changes $\eta S^{-2}$
in a way that one scalar rate cannot undo. This algebraic distinction does
not imply that a normalization rule has lower risk after rate selection.

Write $e_k=\E[\beta_k-\beta^*]$ and
$V_k=\E[(\beta_k-\beta^*)(\beta_k-\beta^*)^\top]$. Fresh sampling gives
\begin{equation}
 V_k=e_ke_k^\top+A_k+\sigma^2N_k,
 \label{eq:conference-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $A_k\succeq0$ is the covariance of the noiseless process under random
feature sampling and $\sigma^2N_k\succeq0$ is the propagated observation-noise
covariance. Appendix~\ref{sec:comparison-proofs} derives the exact recurrence.
This separation distinguishes incomplete learning from two sources of
training variance.

For a subgroup $G$, define excess half squared prediction risks
\begin{equation}
 R_k=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(CV_k),\qquad
 R_{G,k}=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(C_GV_k),
 \label{eq:conference-risks}
\end{equation}
where $C_G=\E[xx^\top\mid G]$ is a conditional second moment, including
the conditional feature mean. Both risks exclude irreducible test noise.
The distribution of training data is unchanged when we evaluate either
objective on the same trained predictor.

\paragraph{A feasible binary model.}
Let $I$ be common and $J$ rare, with
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Prob(I=1)=\tfrac12,\qquad \Prob(J=1)=q,\\
 \Prob(I=1,J=1)=\tfrac{q(1+\ell)}2,
 \end{gathered}
\]
where $0<q<1/2$ and $-1\leq\ell\leq1$. All four cell probabilities are
nonnegative. For $x=(I-1/2,J-q)^\top$, write
$a=1/4$, $b=q(1-q)$, and $c=q\ell/2$. Then
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\c&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_R=\begin{pmatrix}
 a&\ell(1-q)/2\\\ell(1-q)/2&(1-q)^2
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
where $R$ denotes the subgroup $J=1$. A rare-coordinate error of one
contributes $(1-q)^2/2$ to rare risk and $q(1-q)/2$ to aggregate risk.

We compare unscaled features (U) with standard-deviation scaling (S):
\begin{align*}
 P_U&=I_2,& \eta_U&=4h,\\
 P_S&=\diag(a^{-1},b^{-1}),& \eta_S&=h,
\end{align*}
where $P=S^{-2}$. Both choices match the common-coordinate diagonal drift
at $h$. Under independence, they match its entire mean trajectory. Under
dependence, cross-coordinate drift generally prevents that match. This
declared calibration is a controlled comparison, without rate selection or
iterate averaging.

\paragraph{Damped scaling.}
The sampled-data experiments also include damped scaling, with
\begin{equation}
 s_j=\sqrt{v_j+0.01},\qquad P_{jj}=\frac{1}{v_j+0.01},
 \label{eq:conference-damped}
\end{equation}
where $v_j$ is the population variance when known and the training variance
when estimated. The offset $0.01$ is a prespecified heuristic, inherited from
the controlled pilot and fixed in variance units before evaluation. It is not
a theoretically derived or validation-selected value. At a common learning
rate, damping multiplies the SD original-coordinate update by
$v_j/(v_j+0.01)$: approximately one half at binary prevalence $0.01$, but
$0.96$ for a balanced binary feature. This describes its effect, rather than
establishing an optimal damping level.
