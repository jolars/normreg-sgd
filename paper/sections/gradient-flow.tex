\section{Dependence during early stopping}\label{sec:flow}

Gradient flow in the scaled coordinates starts from $\theta(0)=0$ and
satisfies $\dot\theta=S^{-1}(z-CS^{-1}\theta)$. Transforming back gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 \dot\beta=P(z-C\beta),\qquad
 \beta(T)=(I-e^{-TPC})C^{-1}z.
\end{equation}
For the population model, $z=C\beta^*$, so
$\beta(T)=(I-e^{-TPC})\beta^*$.
Although $PC$ need not be symmetric, it is similar to the symmetric positive
definite matrix $S^{-1}CS^{-1}$. Thus the empirical trajectory tends to
$C^{-1}z$ as $T\to\infty$. In this full-rank model scaling changes the path,
while the converged estimator is ordinary least squares.

\begin{proposition}[A transient rare-feature contribution]\label{prop:flow-limit}
Consider population gradient flow from zero for
\[
 C_b=\begin{pmatrix}a&c_b\\c_b&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a>0,\quad b\downarrow0,\quad c_b/b\to\kappa,
\]
with $C_b$ positive definite and fixed $\beta^*\in\R^2$.
Use standard-deviation scaling, so $P_b=\diag(1/a,1/b)$.
For each fixed $T\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
 \beta_{b,1}(T)&\longrightarrow(1-e^{-T})\beta_1^*,\\
 \beta_{b,2}(T)&\longrightarrow(1-e^{-T})\beta_2^*
                         +\kappa T e^{-T}\beta_1^*.
\end{align*}
The convergence is uniform on bounded intervals of $T$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The generator satisfies
\[
 P_bC_b\longrightarrow
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\kappa&1\end{pmatrix}=I+N,
 \qquad N^2=0.
\]
Continuity of the matrix exponential, uniformly on bounded time intervals,
gives $e^{-TP_bC_b}\to e^{-TL}=e^{-T}(I-TN)$.
Substitute this expression into \eqref{eq:flow} with $z=C_b\beta^*$.
\end{proof}

For binary features with fixed common prevalence $q_1\in(0,1)$ and rare
prevalence $q_2\downarrow0$, write
\[
 a=q_1(1-q_1),\quad b=q_2(1-q_2),\quad c=p_{11}-q_1q_2,
 \quad \max(0,q_1+q_2-1)\le p_{11}\le\min(q_1,q_2).
\]
These bounds imply $c=O(b)$ and hence Pearson correlation tends to zero.
In the feasible nested design $p_{11}=q_2$, however,
$c/b\to1-q_1$. If $\beta_2^*=0$, a nonzero rare coefficient can consequently
appear during training, with limiting value
$(1-q_1)T e^{-T}\beta_1^*$. This is a transient coefficient contribution,
not lasso selection or an asymptotic inconsistency claim. It vanishes as
$T\to\infty$ in the limiting trajectory.

The proposition concerns population dynamics at fixed time. It does not
provide a joint theorem for increasing sample size, changing stopping times,
or stochastic updates. In particular, faster population dynamics do not
establish better performance at a fixed number of processed observations.

\subsection{A fixed common block and one rare predictor}

The same mechanism extends to several common predictors. Let
$x_n=(U_n-\E U_n,W_n-q_n)$, where $U_n\in\R^d$ has finite second moments,
$d$ is fixed, and $W_n\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_n)$. The index $n$
labels population distributions; it does not by itself denote an observed
sample size. Consider
$y_n=x_n^\top\beta^*+\varepsilon_n$ with fixed
$\beta^*=(\beta_C^*,\beta_R^*)$, $\E(\varepsilon_n\mid x_n)=0$, and
$\E\varepsilon_n^2<\infty$. Use the population averaged squared loss
$\E(y_n-x_n^\top\beta)^2/2$, known population centering, and deterministic
scales fixed during each trajectory. The centered response has mean zero;
there is no evolving intercept. Subscripts $C$ and $R$ denote the common
block and the rare coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[Common-block population limit]\label{prop:block-flow-limit}
Suppose $q_n\in(0,1)$, $q_n\downarrow0$, and
\[
 C_n=\E x_nx_n^\top
   =\begin{pmatrix}A_n&c_n\\c_n^\top&b_n\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_n\to A\succ0,\quad b_n=q_n(1-q_n),\quad c_n/b_n\to\kappa\in\R^d.
\]
Write $S_n=\diag(D_n,s_{R,n})$, where the common scale matrix $D_n$ is
positive diagonal and $s_{R,n}>0$, and assume
\[
 D_n\to D\succ0,\qquad \frac{b_n}{s_{R,n}^2}\to\rho\in[0,\infty).
\]
Let the deterministic original-scale initial coefficients satisfy
$\beta_n(0)\to\beta^0$, and put
$d_C=\beta_C^*-\beta_C^0$, $d_R=\beta_R^*-\beta_R^0$, and $G=D^{-2}A$.
For every finite $T_0$, the population trajectories converge uniformly on
$0\le T\le T_0$ to
\begin{align}
 \overline\beta_C(T)
   &=\beta_C^*-e^{-TG}d_C,\notag\\
 \overline\beta_R(T)
   &=\beta_R^*-e^{-\rho T}d_R
     +\rho\int_0^T e^{-\rho(T-u)}\kappa^\top e^{-uG}d_C\,du.
     \label{eq:block-flow-limit}
\end{align}
The limit uses original-scale coefficients throughout. Standard-deviation
scaling gives
\[
 D=\diag(\sqrt{A_{11}},\ldots,\sqrt{A_{dd}}),\qquad \rho=1.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The moment assumptions give population score $C_n\beta^*$. Thus, with
$L_n=S_n^{-2}C_n$, the original-scale solution for general initialization is
\[
 \beta_n(T)=\beta^*+e^{-TL_n}\{\beta_n(0)-\beta^*\}.
\]
The blocks satisfy
\[
 L_n=\begin{pmatrix}
       D_n^{-2}A_n&D_n^{-2}c_n\\
       s_{R,n}^{-2}c_n^\top&s_{R,n}^{-2}b_n
     \end{pmatrix}
 \longrightarrow
 L=\begin{pmatrix}G&0\\\rho\kappa^\top&\rho\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Indeed $c_n=b_n\{\kappa+o(1)\}\to0$, and the lower-left block is
$(b_n/s_{R,n}^2)(c_n/b_n)^\top$.
For an induced matrix norm choose a finite $M$ bounding
$\|L_n\|$ and $\|L\|$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The exponential
difference identity gives
\[
 \sup_{0\le T\le T_0}\|e^{-TL_n}-e^{-TL}\|
 \le T_0 e^{MT_0}\|L_n-L\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Together with $\beta_n(0)\to\beta^0$, this proves uniform convergence.
In the limiting triangular system, the common deficit is $e^{-TG}d_C$.
Variation of constants in
\[
 \dot{\overline\beta}_R
   =\rho\{\beta_R^*-\overline\beta_R+
                    \kappa^\top e^{-TG}d_C\}
\]
then yields \eqref{eq:block-flow-limit}.
\end{proof}

The convergence assumption concerns original-scale initialization. A fixed
nonzero initial rare coefficient in scaled coordinates generally fails it
under standard-deviation scaling because $s_{R,n}\to0$. Zero initialization
in either coordinate system satisfies it. The covariance assumptions also
imply eventual positive definiteness: the Schur complement is
$b_n-c_n^\top A_n^{-1}c_n=b_n\{1-O(b_n)\}>0$.

\paragraph{Convolution and common learning rates.}
The last term in \eqref{eq:block-flow-limit} is a convolution of the remaining
common error with the rare-coordinate response. It vanishes if the common
coefficients start at their signal values or if $\kappa=0$. Its sign can
change over time when different common modes contribute with opposite signs.
To display these modes, let
\[
 D^{-1}AD^{-1}v_j=\lambda_jv_j,\qquad
 v_i^\top v_j=\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}},\qquad \lambda_j>0.
\]
Since $G=D^{-1}(D^{-1}AD^{-1})D$, the transferred term equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-flow-modes}
 \rho\sum_{j=1}^d
  (\kappa^\top D^{-1}v_j)(v_j^\top Dd_C)K_{\rho,\lambda_j}(T),
 \qquad
 K_{\rho,\lambda}(T)=
 \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{e^{-\lambda T}-e^{-\rho T}}{\rho-\lambda},&\lambda\ne\rho,\\[6pt]
  T e^{-\rho T},&\lambda=\rho.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This follows by integrating
$e^{-\rho(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_j u}$ for each eigenvector. Equal common and rare
rates yield the $T e^{-\rho T}$ factor without requiring an inverse of
$G-\rho I$. With one common predictor, standard-deviation scaling gives
$G=1$ and $\rho=1$. Zero initialization therefore recovers
Proposition~\ref{prop:flow-limit}. With a correlated common block, marginal
standardization need not give $G=I$, and several decay rates remain.

If $\rho>0$, all terms in \eqref{eq:block-flow-modes} vanish as
$T\to\infty$, after taking the population rarity limit. If $\rho=0$, the
limiting rare coefficient stays at $\beta_R^0$ for bounded $T$. In particular,
no scaling gives $D=I$ and $\rho=0$, while scaling the rare coordinate by
its standard deviation gives $\rho=1$. Rare variance scaling,
$s_{R,n}=b_n$, has divergent $b_n/s_{R,n}^2$ and lies outside the proposition.

\paragraph{Feasible dependence for binary common predictors.}
For any integrable common vector, the exact identity
\[
 \frac{c_n}{b_n}
   =\E(U_n\mid W_n=1)-\E(U_n\mid W_n=0)
\]
follows by conditioning on $W_n$. If $U_n\in\{0,1\}^d$, each component is
bounded in absolute value by one. Feasibility still requires a nonnegative
joint law on the entire binary support, not only pairwise covariance bounds.
For example, fix a law $\pi(u)>0$ on all $u\in\{0,1\}^d$, and any probability
law $\nu$ on the same support. For all sufficiently small $q_n$, define
\[
 \Prob(U_n=u,W_n=1)=q_n\nu(u),\qquad
 \Prob(U_n=u,W_n=0)=\pi(u)-q_n\nu(u)\ge0.
\]
It suffices to take $q_n\le\min_{u:\nu(u)>0}\pi(u)/\nu(u)$. The common
marginal is $\pi$, its covariance $A$ is positive definite, and
\[
 \frac{c_n}{b_n}=
 \frac{\E_\nu U-\E_\pi U}{1-q_n}
 \longrightarrow\kappa=\E_\nu U-\E_\pi U.
\]
Taking $\nu=\pi$ gives independence. A point mass at the all-ones or all-zeros
cell gives nested or disjoint rare presence, respectively, while other laws
allow mixed associations. In all these examples, common--rare Pearson
correlations are $O(\sqrt{b_n})$ and vanish even when $\kappa\ne0$.

The deterministic script \texttt{experiments/check\_block\_flow.py} checks
the formulas using such full joint supports with three correlated common
binary predictors. It compares matrix exponentials against independently
integrated observation gradients in scaled coordinates, checks the spectral
formula against numerical convolution, and includes equal rates and nonzero
initialization. A sequence with changing common covariance, scales, and
initial values also approaches \eqref{eq:block-flow-limit} on a fixed time
grid. These checks support the algebra; uniform convergence follows from
the proof above.

The result makes no assertion uniform over increasing stopping times $T_n$
or common dimension. It does not justify interchanging rarity and long-time
limits or replacing population covariances by empirical ones. Sample-size
conditions, estimated centering and scales, stochastic updates, and stopping
chosen from data require separate results.
