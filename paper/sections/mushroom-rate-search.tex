\section{A separate Mushroom rate-search follow-up}
\label{sec:mushroom-rate-search}

The frozen experiment in Appendix~\ref{sec:practical} leaves an upper-rate
boundary: all 32 large-sample unscaled aggregate selections use factor $.8$.
The follow-up declares 17 factors before evaluating the new candidates:
\[
 a_0\in\{.001,.01,.05,.2,.4,.6,.8,1,1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8,2,2.4,3.2,4.8,6.4\},
 \qquad \eta=a_0/\max(L,10^{-12}).
\]
All scalings receive this same search, with the original batches 16 and 128,
both sample sizes, all three empirical preprocessing/intercept arms, and the
same partitions and replacement index streams. The old five factors remain
eligible. The primary expansion uses the original stopping checkpoints
$\{0,512,2048,8192\}$; a secondary search adds $\{128,256,1024,4096\}$.
The primary arm centers predictors and fits an intercept. Both aggregate and
rare validation objectives are evaluated, with the original fallback when the
rare validation group is empty. Models are not refitted on validation data.

Each expanded scaling/arm uses 34 paths, or nominal work $34M$ at cap $M$,
versus $10M$ in the nested original search. This is equal effort across scalings
within each search, with greater effort than the original grid. Additional
stopping checkpoints reuse these paths but increase validation scoring.
Curvature calibration, preprocessing, and scoring costs are outside $M$.
The original study and its artifacts are preserved. This is a declared follow-up
on already studied data, not independent confirmation on new data.

Table~\ref{tab:mushroom-expanded-gaps} separates effects that persist from
those no longer resolved after rate adjustment. At the larger size, aggregate
selection gives unscaled aggregate loss $.001341\ (.000074)$ and rare loss
$.005955\ (.000888)$, compared with $.001754\ (.000060)$ and
$.012482\ (.001386)$ in the original search. SD's rare advantage is now
within two MCSE of zero. Damping retains lower aggregate and rare loss,
$.000810\ (.000039)$ and $.000682\ (.000133)$. At the smaller size,
aggregate-selected rare losses are $.098799\ (.032694)$, $.076035\ (.028437)$,
and $.078161\ (.028979)$ for none, SD, and damping. Pairing resolves
differences that marginal error bars alone do not describe.

Rare-objective selection still has costs. At $n=2048$, switching the expanded
SD search from aggregate to rare validation reduces rare loss by
$.002507\ (.000951)$ but raises aggregate loss by $.136592\ (.079298)$.
The rare-selected aggregate mean is $.141068$, median $.004357$, and maximum
$2.36513$. The corresponding unscaled mean is $.039395$, median $.002703$,
and maximum $.509963$. Extreme finite outcomes remain in every mean.

The expanded unscaled green coefficient averages $.59990$, versus $.48216$
before expansion; SD and damping average $.77633$ and $.91262$.
Figure~\ref{fig:mushroom-expanded-paths} shows both risks alongside these
coefficient paths. The centered green contributions are $.59460$, $.76948$,
and $.90456$, while other subgroup contributions are $-.14755$, $-.30233$,
and $-.40205$. Centered intercepts are approximately $.485$.
Full one-hot coefficients remain optimizer-dependent in a rank-deficient design.

All 32 expanded unscaled aggregate selections in this primary comparison use
$B=16$, $M=8192$, and factors $.6$--$1.8$. Every immediate higher tested
neighbor at the same batch and stop has worse validation loss; the smallest
gap is $9.57\times10^{-7}$. No primary selection uses the new upper factor
$6.4$. This resolves the original edge within the declared grid. It does not
prove a continuous optimum or equal approximation error across scaling rules.
The separate uncentered rare-objective search with extra early stops retains
49 upper-edge decisions across sample sizes and budget caps, so those
comparisons remain unresolved at the boundary.

Shorter budgets retain stronger SD rare advantages. At large-sample caps 512
and 2048, the primary aggregate-selected gaps are
$-.072857\ (.011815)$ and $-.012679\ (.004061)$.
At cap 8192, fixing the centered intercept gives a gap
$-.000925\ (.001250)$, while uncentered fitted-intercept training gives
$-.038224\ (.004383)$ under the original stopping grid.
The longest-budget centered result does not extend to all training geometries.

The archives retain 156,672 endpoints and 20,736 selection decisions.
There are 2950 failed saved endpoints from 1798 trajectories, with first-failure
updates recorded. Failure means nonfinite arithmetic or a coefficient/centered
intercept exceeding $10^{100}$; all later endpoints remain flagged. Finite
unstable candidates stay eligible. No all-failed selection fallback is needed.
Five small-sample training sets lack the green category, and thirteen validation
sets lack it. The corresponding aggregate fallback is explicit and does not
provide subgroup information. Under expanded aggregate selection, mean rare
loss on the five absent-training splits is $.51233$, $.43994$, and $.44841$
for none, SD, and damping.

The protocol, declaration, and complete summaries are stored separately from
the frozen study. Independent checks reconstruct all losses, selections,
paired summaries, coefficient contributions, and boundary diagnostics; replay
all paths for replication zero of each size and every selected path for the
remaining splits in transformed coordinates; and verify the nested original
grid against the frozen archive. Source, dependency, data, archive, figure,
and preservation hashes are checked. Human review of interpretation remains
part of manuscript preparation.
