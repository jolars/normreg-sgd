\section{Estimated preprocessing and validation selection}
\label{sec:practical}

We next test which aspects of the controlled comparisons remain observable with
sampled designs, estimated preprocessing, and validation-based selection. This
is a separate finite-grid experiment; it does not extend the population
theorems to estimated scales. The complete protocol, results, and reproducible
artifacts are recorded in \texttt{notes/practical-selection.md} and
\texttt{notes/practical-selection-results.md}.

The synthetic stage uses eight feasible binary laws, 128 independent
replications per law, 256 training observations, and 128 independent validation
observations. It includes independent and nested signals at $q=0.02$ and
$q=0.002$, opposite-sign signals under nested and disjoint dependence, and
rare-only and common-only signals. We compare known preprocessing with
estimated scales, training centering with a fixed or fitted intercept, and an
uncentered fit with an intercept. Estimated variances use denominator $n$.
Constant training columns receive divisor one and coefficient zero; absent
features cannot acquire a fitted effect in these estimated arms. All fitted
intercepts start at the training response mean. Population-oracle arms instead
start at the true centered intercept zero. SGD revisits a fixed noisy training
sample with replacement, preserving its observation noise.

The divisors are $1$, $\sqrt{v_j}$, and $\sqrt{v_j+0.01}$. Each scaling and
preprocessing arm receives five rates
$\eta=a/\max(L,10^{-12})$, $a\in\{0.001,0.01,0.05,0.2,0.8\}$, and two
batch sizes. Here $L$ is empirical largest curvature, including the unscaled
intercept coordinate when fitted. This calibration is not a stochastic
stability guarantee. Synthetic batches are $B\in\{1,16\}$ with observation
caps $M\in\{128,512,2048\}$. We retain initialization and every checkpoint,
and compare fixed $a=0.05$, $B=16$ with validation selection of rate/batch or
rate/batch/stopping. Both aggregate and rare-presence validation half squared
loss are used. If the rare validation group is empty, its rule explicitly
falls back to aggregate loss. No test outcome enters selection, and no selected
model is refitted on validation data. Ten trajectories per scaling/arm cost
$10M$ processed observations at cap $M$, with checkpoints sharing paths.

Relative learning remains visible under practical preprocessing. For the
independent $(1,1)$ signal at $q=0.02$ and noise variance three, the fixed
configuration's SD-minus-none rare excess half squared loss is $-0.1653$
(paired MCSE $0.0295$) at $M=128$ and $+0.0901$ ($0.0697$) at $M=2048$
with training centering and a fitted intercept. The early advantage is clear;
the later disadvantage is within two MCSE of zero. At the latter budget, the
rare-only signal favors SD by $0.2320$ ($0.0233$), whereas the common-only
signal favors no scaling by $0.1272$ ($0.0159$). These observations concern
the declared sampled-data configurations, rather than the exact calibrations
of the population comparisons.

Missing groups impose a more direct limitation. At $q=0.002$, the two anchor
laws have no rare validation observations in 106 and 105 of 128 replications.
Validation can then select a finite but explosive rare coefficient because
its evaluation contains no rare cases. One centered, SD-scaled fit has rare
coefficient about $-6.719\times10^{16}$ and validation half squared loss
$2.086$, yet its exact-cell rare loss is about $2.257\times10^{33}$.
This path remains in the results. A separate 160-digit decimal investigation
replays every eligible trajectory for the worst selected replication in each
of 24 law/scaling panels; all 24 selections remain unchanged. Thus the
observed failure is not explained by the detected floating-point cancellation.
Extreme finite-grid means are tail dominated and are not precise population
risk estimates. The 151 failed synthetic endpoints also remain flagged in the
archive.

\paragraph{A sparse categorical task.}
We fit a squared-loss linear predictor to the recorded poisonous label in the
UCI Mushroom dataset \citep{uciMushroom}, using all 22 categorical predictors.
The declared subgroup is green spore-print color: 72 of 8124 records.
These are hypothetical descriptions derived from a field guide, so random-record
evaluation does not establish generalization to new species or field data.
There are no duplicate predictor rows. Vocabulary, centering, and scales use
training data alone, missingness is a category, and unknown categories map to
zero blocks. The one-hot design uses sparse storage; centering is implemented
algebraically. Full one-hot coefficients are optimizer-dependent in this
rank-deficient design, not uniquely identified effects.

We use 32 random partitions for each training/validation size $(256,128)$ and
$(2048,1024)$; remaining records are held out for testing. All candidates share
partitions and replacement index streams. Batches are $B\in\{16,128\}$ and
caps are $M\in\{512,2048,8192\}$. Predictions are not clipped. Table~\ref{tab:practical}
reports the centered/fitted arm at the largest cap, with joint rate, batch,
and stopping selection. Other arms, shorter caps, fixed stopping, all paths,
and the three-times-larger search over scaling rules are retained separately.

Under aggregate selection, the SD-minus-none rare contrast is $-0.00657$
(paired MCSE $0.00153$); the aggregate contrast is $+0.002863$ ($0.00213$).
Changing SD selection from the aggregate to the rare objective reduces rare
loss by $0.002754$ ($0.000967$), with a variable aggregate penalty of
$0.11929$ ($0.0756$). The original-scale green coefficient averages
$0.4822$, $0.7462$, and $0.7678$ for none, SD, and damped scaling under
aggregate selection. Other categorical coefficients partly offset its larger
contribution. The mechanism survives as a finite-budget empirical effect,
while its practical ranking depends on objective and tuning.

The smaller sample has no green training observations in five partitions and
none in validation in thirteen. At the larger size, every no-scaling aggregate
selection chooses the largest rate factor, so the search does not resolve its
continuous optimum. These coverage and grid limits preclude a general
normalization recommendation. Independent checks reconstruct every archived
validation/test loss and selection, paired uncertainty, and representative
complete paths in transformed coordinates. The earlier controlled studies
and their reported failures remain unchanged.
