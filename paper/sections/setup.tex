\section{Setup and conventions}

For a fixed sample, profile out an unpenalized intercept and let $X_c$ and
$y_c$ denote the centered design and response. Write
\[
 C=X_c^\top X_c/n,\qquad z=X_c^\top y_c/n,\qquad
 y_c=X_c\beta^*+\varepsilon_c.
\]
The empirical loss is $\beta^\top C\beta/2-z^\top\beta$, up to a constant.
Unless stated otherwise, $C$ is positive definite. Independent Gaussian
errors with variance $\sigma_\varepsilon^2>0$ give, conditional on the design,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-law}
 z\sim N(C\beta^*,\tau^2C),\qquad \tau^2=\sigma_\varepsilon^2/n.
\end{equation}

Population calculations instead use a centered random feature vector $x$
with covariance $C=\E xx^\top$ and response
$y=x^\top\beta^*+\varepsilon$, where $\E(\varepsilon\mid x)=0$ and
$\E\varepsilon^2<\infty$.
The population excess squared loss is
$\E\{x^\top(\beta-\beta^*)\}^2/2$.
We state explicitly whether an argument uses this population model, a fixed
empirical design, or fresh independent observations for stochastic updates.
Population centering and scales are known in the initial stochastic
calculation. Estimating them and updating an intercept require separate
analysis.

Let $S=\diag(s_1,\ldots,s_p)$ with positive scales fixed during training.
Dividing features by their scales gives $\widetilde x=S^{-1}x$ and
coefficient $\theta=S\beta$. All reported coefficients are $\beta$, on the
original scale. Write $P=S^{-2}$ for the induced preconditioner. No scaling,
standard-deviation scaling, and variance scaling correspond to
$s_j=C_{jj}^{\delta}$ for $\delta=0,1/2,1$, respectively.

We use $T\ge0$ for gradient-flow time, $k$ for update count, $B$ for batch
size, and $\eta>0$ for a constant learning rate in stochastic updates.
For discrete gradient descent, $T=k\eta$ is its continuous-time calibration,
not a measure of processed observations. An online run of $k$ batches uses
$kB$ observations. The symbols $t=\lambda_1/n$ and $r=\lambda_2/n$ remain
reserved for the averaged lasso and ridge penalties in the appendices.
