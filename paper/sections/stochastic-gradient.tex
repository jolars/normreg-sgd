\section{Rare observations and stochastic stability}
\label{sec:sgd}

Unit marginal variance does not control the size of individual stochastic
updates. Throughout this section, centering and positive diagonal scales are
known and fixed, the intercept is fixed at its population value, and each batch
contains fresh independent observations independent of the past. The loss is
averaged squared loss. These assumptions exclude empirical scaling, fixed-data
resampling, and adaptive preconditioners.

\begin{proposition}[Scalar batch stability]
 \label{prop:sgd-stability}
 Let $X \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)$ with $0 < q < 1$, and set
 \[
  Z = \frac{X - q}{\sqrt{q(1 - q)}},\qquad \mu_4
    = \E Z^4
    = \frac{(1 - q)^3 + q^3}{q(1 - q)}.
 \]
 Fit the noiseless model $Y = Z\theta^*$ by SGD on squared loss
 $(Z\theta - Y)^2/2$, with constant step size $\eta$ and batches of size
 $B \ge 1$. Each batch consists of fresh independent observations and is
 independent of the previous iterates. For a deterministic initial error
 $e_0 = \theta_0 - \theta^*$,
 \[
  \E e_k^2 = \left[ 1 - 2\eta + \eta^2\left\{ 1 + \frac{\mu_4 - 1}{B} \right\} \right]^k e_0^2.
 \]
 For $e_0 \ne 0$, the expected squared error tends to zero precisely when
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sgd-stability}
  0 < \eta < \frac{2}{1 + (\mu_4 - 1)/B}.
 \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
 Write $H_k = B^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^B Z_{ki}^2$. The update gives
 $e_{k+1} = (1 - \eta H_k)e_k$. Since $\E Z^2 = 1$, independence within each
 batch gives $\E H_k = 1$ and $\E H_k^2 = 1 + (\mu_4 - 1)/B$. Independence from
 the past yields the displayed recurrence for the second moment. Its nonnegative
 multiplicative factor is less than one exactly under \eqref{eq:sgd-stability}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scalar rates and observation cost}

Write $a_{q,B} = 1 + (\mu_4 - 1)/B$. Completing the square in the recurrence
gives the unique optimal constant step size and its mean-square factor:
\[
 \eta_* = a_{q,B}^{-1},\qquad r_*
        = 1 - a_{q,B}^{-1},\qquad r(\eta)
        = r_* + a_{q,B}(\eta - \eta_*)^2.
\]
For $q \ne 1/2$, the exact exponential contraction per processed observation at
this step size is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:scalar-observation-rate}
 \ell_{q,B} = -\frac{\log r_*}{B}
            = \frac1B\log\left( 1 + \frac{B}{\mu_4 - 1} \right),\qquad \E e_k^2
            = e^{-kB\ell_{q,B}}e_0^2.
\end{equation}
The special case $q = 1/2$ has $Z^2 = 1$ almost surely and reaches zero error in
one update with $\eta = 1$. As $q \downarrow 0$, $\mu_4 \sim q^{-1}$, so
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
             Bq             &    \eta_{\max} = 2/a_{q,B}     &            \eta_*             &          r_*           &              \ell_{q,B}               \\
  \hline
            \to 0           &            \sim 2Bq            &            \sim Bq            &    1 - Bq(1 + o(1))    &                \sim q                 \\
  \to \gamma \in (0,\infty) & \to \frac{2\gamma}{1 + \gamma} & \to \frac{\gamma}{1 + \gamma} & \to \frac1{1 + \gamma} & \sim q\frac{\log(1 + \gamma)}{\gamma} \\
         \to \infty         &             \to 2              &             \to 1             &     \sim (Bq)^{-1}     &        \sim \frac{\log(Bq)}{B}
 \end{array}
\]
For fixed $q \ne 1/2$, $\ell_{q,B}$ decreases with $B$: differentiate
$\log(1 + t)/t$ and use $\log(1 + t) > t/(1 + t)$ for $t > 0$. Thus larger
batches improve this scalar noiseless contraction per update, but do not improve
its optimized exponential rate per observation. This comparison concerns whole
batches and constant step sizes, with no claim about wall-clock time or optimal
tuning under observation noise. The original coefficient is
$\beta = \theta/\sqrt{q(1 - q)}$, so its squared error is $e_k^2/[q(1 - q)]$ and
has the same contraction factor.

This calculation also distinguishes mean convergence from mean-square
convergence: $\E e_k = (1 - \eta)^k e_0$, which can converge while the second
moment grows. A covariance-only description of the population loss therefore
does not determine stochastic stability.

\subsection{An exact multivariate criterion}

The generalized-eigenvalue criterion below is the stochastic least-squares form
of the general criterion of \citet[Theorems~5 and~14]{mulayoff2024exact}. Their
independent batches contain distinct examples from a fixed dataset and use the
correction $(n - B)/\{B(n - 1)\}$; independent draws here use $1/B$. Our strict
interval concerns decay for every initialization, whereas boundedness at the
endpoint is a different conclusion. The subsequent rare-prevalence limits
evaluate this established criterion.

In one dimension, rescaling a feature can be absorbed into the learning rate.
Several feature frequencies instead impose constraints on relative updates. Let
$z = S^{-1}x$, $H = \E zz^\top \succ 0$, and $u_k = S(\beta_k - \beta^*)$,
assuming $\E\|z\|^4 < \infty$. For a generic batch put
$\widehat H = B^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^Bz_i z_i^\top$. On the real symmetric matrices,
with Frobenius inner product, define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal M(U)   & = \E[zz^\top Uzz^\top],                                               & \mathcal A(U)   & = HU + UH,                                       \\
 \mathcal B_B(U) & = \E[\widehat H U\widehat H] = (1 - B^{-1})HUH + B^{-1}\mathcal M(U), & \mathcal T_B(U) & = U - \eta\mathcal A(U) + \eta^2\mathcal B_B(U).
\end{align*}
Equivalently, with $R = I - \eta H$,
$\mathcal T_B(U) = RUR + \eta^2\{\mathcal M(U) - HUH\}/B$. These are the
second-moment operators for fresh-batch SGD. All operator eigenvalues below
refer to this symmetric-matrix space, not just diagonal matrices.

\begin{proposition}[Multivariate mean-square stability]
 \label{prop:matrix-stability}
 For noiseless SGD, convergence $\E\|u_k\|^2 \to 0$ for every deterministic
 initial error is equivalent to $\rho(\mathcal T_B) < 1$. More explicitly, the
 exact interval is $0 < \eta < \eta_{\mathrm{crit}}$, where
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:operator-critical-step}
  \eta_{\mathrm{crit}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max} (\mathcal A^{-1/2}\mathcal B_B\mathcal A^{-1/2})}.
 \end{equation}
 If a coordinate of $z$ is the standardized Bernoulli variable of
 Proposition~\ref{prop:sgd-stability}, then, with
 $K_B = \E\widehat H^2 = (1 - B^{-1})H^2 + B^{-1}\E[\|z\|^2zz^\top]$,
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:matrix-step-bounds}
  \frac{2\lambda_{\min}(H)}{\lambda_{\max}(K_B)} \ 
  \le \ \eta_{\mathrm{crit}} \ 
  \le \ \frac{2}{1 + (\mu_4 - 1)/B}.
 \end{equation}
 The left endpoint is a sufficient strict upper bound on a chosen step size; the
 right endpoint is a necessary strict upper bound. Equality in either bound does
 not itself establish stability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
 Independence yields $\E[u_k u_k^\top] = \mathcal T_B^k(u_0u_0^\top)$. Rank-one
 positive semidefinite matrices span the symmetric matrices, so decay for every
 $u_0$ is equivalent to decay of powers of this finite-dimensional operator, or
 $\rho(\mathcal T_B) < 1$. The operator is self-adjoint. For symmetric $U$, let
 $|U|$ replace its eigenvalues by their absolute values. In an eigenbasis of
 $U$, for each symmetric update matrix $F = I - \eta\widehat H$,
 \[
  |\langle U,FUF\rangle| = \left| \sum_{i,j}\lambda_i(U)\lambda_j(U)F_{ij}^2 \right|
                         \le \langle |U|,F|U|F\rangle.
 \]
 Averaging and maximizing Rayleigh quotients shows
 $\rho(\mathcal T_B) = \lambda_{\max}(\mathcal T_B)$. Further, $\mathcal A$ is
 positive definite because
 $\langle U,\mathcal A(U)\rangle = 2\operatorname{tr}(HU^2) > 0$ for $U \ne 0$,
 while $\mathcal B_B$ is positive semidefinite because
 $\langle U,\mathcal B_B(U)\rangle = \E\|\widehat H^{1/2}U\widehat H^{1/2}\|_F^2 \ge 0$.
 For $\eta > 0$, $I - \mathcal T_B$ is positive definite precisely when
 $\mathcal A - \eta\mathcal B_B$ is, which gives
 \eqref{eq:operator-critical-step}.

 For the sufficient bound, conditional on $u_k = u$,
 \[
  \E[\|u_{k+1}\|^2\mid u_k=u]
  = u^\top(I - 2\eta H + \eta^2K_B)u
  \le[1-2\eta\lambda_{\min}(H)+\eta^2\lambda_{\max}(K_B)] \|u\|^2.
 \]
 For necessity use $U = e_re_r^\top$, where $r$ is the rare coordinate. Its
 Rayleigh quotient for $\mathcal T_B$ equals
 $\E(1 - \eta\widehat H_{rr})^2 = 1 - 2\eta + \eta^2a_{q,B}$. It must be
 strictly less than one. This proves the bounds without assuming that the rare
 coordinate evolves independently.
\end{proof}

Dependence enters through both $H$ and $\mathcal M$: its entries include
$\E[z_i z_j z_l z_m]$. For a common coordinate $U_i$ and rare coordinate $Z$,
these include $\E[U_iU_jZ^2]$ and $\E[U_iZ^3]$, as well as $\mu_4 = \E Z^4$.
Covariance and the scalar fourth moment do not specify the full operator. For
example, if $X$ has common prevalence $p$, $J$ has rare prevalence $q$, and
$J \le X$ almost surely, put $U = (X - p)/\sqrt{p(1 - p)}$ and
$Z = (J - q)/\sqrt{q(1 - q)}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:nested-mixed-fourth}
 \E[UZ^3] = \frac{(1 - p)\{(1 - q)^3 + q^3\}}
 {\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\sqrt q\,(1 - q)^{3/2}},\qquad \E[U^2Z^2]
          \longrightarrow \frac{1 - p}{p}.
\end{equation}
The identity follows from $(J - q)^3 = (1 - 3q + 3q^2)J - q^3$, $\E U = 0$, and
$\E[UJ] = q(1 - p)/\sqrt{p(1 - p)}$. The limit follows by conditioning on $J$:
rare observations have $X = 1$, while the $J = 0$ contribution tends to zero. In
particular, $\E[UZ^3]$ is of order $q^{-1/2}$ even though $\E UZ = O(\sqrt q)$.
The finite-support checks verify the exact expression. These examples illustrate
mixed moments. Section~\ref{sec:sampling} gives a separate matched-covariance
binary comparison.

\subsection{A bounded common block and a rare binary feature}

\begin{proposition}[Batch-rarity orders]
 \label{prop:block-stability-orders}
 Let $x_q = (x_{c,q}^\top,J_q - q)^\top$ be centered, with a fixed number of
 common coordinates, $J_q \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)$, and
 $q \downarrow 0$. Assume $\Cov(x_{c,q}) \to A \succ 0$ and $\|x_{c,q}\| \le L$
 almost surely, uniformly in $q$. Use population standard-deviation scales in
 every coordinate. Then, uniformly over integers $B \ge 1$, constants
 independent of $q,B$ give
 \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:batch-rarity-order}
  \eta_{\mathrm{crit}} = \Theta\bigl(\min\{1,Bq\}\bigr).
 \end{equation}
 There are constants $c,c' > 0$ such that $\eta = c\min\{1,Bq\}$ gives
 \[
  \E\|u_k\|^2 \le (1 - c'\min\{1,Bq\})^k\|u_0\|^2
 \]
 in the noiseless model. Thus a fixed relative reduction in this squared norm is
 achievable in $O(1/\min\{1,Bq\})$ updates or $O(\max\{B,q^{-1}\})$ processed
 observations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
 The common scales are bounded above and away from zero. Write the scaled vector
 as $z = (U^\top,Z)^\top$, so $\|U\| \le L_0$ uniformly. Since $\E U = 0$,
 $\E UZ = \sqrt{q/(1 - q)}\E[U\mid J_q=1] = O(\sqrt q)$. The common covariance
 after scaling has a positive definite limit, hence
 $h \le \lambda_{\min}(H) \le \lambda_{\max}(H) \le h_1$ for some $h,h_1 > 0$
 and small $q$. Also
 \[
  \E\|z\|^4 \le L_0^4 + 2L_0^2 + \mu_4
            = O(q^{-1}),\qquad \lambda_{\max}(K_B)
            \le h_1^2 + B^{-1}\E\|z\|^4
            \le C\{1 + (Bq)^{-1}\}.
 \]
 The bounds in \eqref{eq:matrix-step-bounds} prove the order statement. Choose
 $c$ small enough that $\eta\lambda_{\max}(K_B) \le h$. The conditional energy
 inequality in the preceding proof then gives a factor at most $1 - h\eta$.
 Iteration proves the rate and its observation cost.
\end{proof}

This assumption class includes any fixed binary common block whose covariance
has a positive definite limit, with arbitrary feasible dependence on the rare
indicator. If additionally $c_q/\{q(1 - q)\} \to \kappa$, it is consistent with
the population rare-feature limit in Section~\ref{sec:flow}; that limit alone
does not control stochastic fourth moments for unbounded common features. The
order statement gives $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}} = \Theta(Bq)$ when $Bq \to 0$ and
$\Theta(1)$ in the other two regimes. Boundedness also permits exact threshold
limits. Define
\[
 H_c = D^{-1}AD^{-1},\qquad H_0
     = \diag(H_c,1),\qquad D
     = \diag(\sqrt{A_{11}},\ldots,\sqrt{A_{dd}}).
\]
Then the three regimes satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:block-threshold-limits}
 \begin{array}{c|c}
             Bq             &                                             \eta_{\mathrm{crit}}                                             \\
  \hline
            \to 0           &                                                   \sim 2Bq                                                   \\
  \to \gamma \in (0,\infty) & \displaystyle \longrightarrow \min\left\{ \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}(H_c)}, \frac{2\gamma}{1 + \gamma} \right\} \\
         \to \infty         &                         \displaystyle \longrightarrow \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}(H_0)}
 \end{array}
\end{equation}
To prove these limits, let $\Pi_r(U) = U_{rr}e_re_r^\top$. Every entry of
$\mathcal M$ with at most two rare factors is $O(1)$, and those with three are
$O(q^{-1/2})$, whereas $q\mu_4 \to 1$. Thus $q\mathcal M \to \Pi_r$ in operator
norm. Also $H \to H_0$, so $\mathcal A$ converges to the positive definite
operator $\mathcal A_0(U) = H_0U + UH_0$. If $Bq \to 0$,
$Bq\mathcal B_B \to \Pi_r$, and the largest eigenvalue of
$\mathcal A_0^{-1/2}\Pi_r\mathcal A_0^{-1/2}$ is $1/2$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:operator-critical-step} proves the first limit. If
$Bq \to \gamma$, then $B \to \infty$ and
$\mathcal B_B(U) \to H_0UH_0 + \Pi_r(U)/\gamma$. In an eigenbasis of $H_c$, the
rare diagonal mode has threshold $2\gamma/(1 + \gamma)$. The common modes have
thresholds $1/\lambda_i + 1/\lambda_j$, minimized by $2/\lambda_{\max}(H_c)$,
and common--rare modes have thresholds $1 + 1/\lambda_i$. These last thresholds
cannot decrease the displayed minimum: if $\lambda_i \ge 1$ they exceed
$2/\lambda_{\max}(H_c)$, and otherwise they exceed two. Finally, $Bq \to \infty$
makes $\mathcal M/B \to 0$ and yields the population threshold
$2/\lambda_{\max}(H_0)$. Mixed moments affect finite-$q$ stability even though
their contributions vanish in these particular threshold limits.

A shared stable step size can therefore slow the common coordinates. For
$Bq \to 0$, every stable $\eta$ is $O(Bq)$. In the noiseless or conditionally
mean-zero noise model, $\E u_k = R^ku_0$. For a nonzero common-only initial
error $u_0 = (v^\top,0)^\top$ and small $q$, $\|R\| \le 1$ and telescoping gives
\[
 \|[\E u_k]_c - v\| \le k\eta\|H\|\|v\| = O(kBq)\|v\|.
\]
Thus $kB = o(q^{-1})$ cannot remove a fixed fraction of this common mean error.
The common scales stay bounded, so this statement also holds for original-scale
common coefficients. For example, with an independent centered Bernoulli$(1/2)$
common feature, its mean error is exactly $(1 - \eta)^k$ times its initial
error; common-only SGD would permit a constant step, whereas joint mean-square
stability requires $\eta = O(Bq)$. The upper contraction bound concerns
$\|S(\beta_k - \beta^*)\|^2$. Although stability is equivalent in original
coordinates for each fixed $q$, its comparison constants for the rare
coefficient depend on $q$.

\subsection{Observation noise and scope}

If $y = x^\top\beta^* + \varepsilon$, $\E(\varepsilon \mid x) = 0$, and
$Q = \E[\varepsilon^2zz^\top]$ is finite, independence and conditional centering
remove the noise cross terms. At a stable constant step,
\[
 \E[u_ku_k^\top] = \mathcal T_B^k(u_0u_0^\top)
                   + \frac{\eta^2}{B}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal T_B^j(Q)
                 \longrightarrow \frac{\eta^2}{B}(I - \mathcal T_B)^{-1}(Q).
\]
For $Q \ne 0$ this limit is nonzero, so noiseless contraction must not be
reported as noisy error converging to zero. In the scalar homoscedastic case it
is $\eta\sigma_\varepsilon^2/[B(2 - \eta a_{q,B})]$ in scaled coordinates,
divided by $q(1 - q)$ for the original coefficient. Optimizing finite-budget
noisy prediction, unbounded or growing common blocks, and estimated scales
require further assumptions. Section~\ref{sec:sampling} derives the conditional
identities needed for fixed-data sampling. The deterministic checks enumerate
feasible binary supports and batches to verify the operators, mixed moments,
bounds, and batch-rarity limits.
