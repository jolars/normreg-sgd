\section{Variance scaling across practical preprocessing arms}
\label{sec:variance-scaling-followup}

A separately declared follow-up adds variance scaling, $s_j = v_j$
($\delta = 1$), to every preprocessing arm of the sampled binary and Mushroom
studies. It reuses all eight binary laws and 128 replications per law, both
Mushroom sizes and 32 partitions per size, and the original training,
validation, and test samples. Population variances are used only in the
synthetic known-preprocessing arm; other arms use training variances with
denominator $n$. Constant-column, unknown-category, centering, initialization,
and intercept policies are inherited unchanged.

Each of none, SD, damping $.01$, and variance scaling receives the same 25
curvature-relative factors: the earlier 17-factor Mushroom grid, two smaller
factors $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-4}$, and six successive doublings from $12.8$
through $409.6$. With two batch sizes, each rule costs nominally $50M$ processed
observations per arm and split at cap $M$. Aggregate or rare validation jointly
selects rate, batch, and stopping. The primary search uses the original
checkpoints; a secondary search adds intermediate checkpoints. Nested
five-factor practical and 17-factor Mushroom searches retain comparisons at
their earlier tuning effort. Test outcomes never select a configuration or alter
the grid. Initialization, empty-group fallbacks, numerical failures, and extreme
finite selections are retained.

The original motivation for variance scaling under lasso concerns penalty
weights on the original coefficient scale. In this experiment there is no
explicit penalty. The active-coordinate SGD multiplier is
$\eta/s_j^2 = \eta/v_j^2$, and empirical curvature calibration changes $\eta$ as
well. The comparison therefore concerns relative learning, noise, and stability
under a finite budget; a shrinkage motivation supplies no tuned prediction
ordering.

\paragraph{Mushroom results and boundaries.}

At $n = 2048$, $M = 8192$, aggregate selection, and original stops, the
centered/fitted variance-scaled predictor has aggregate loss $.311988$ (MCSE
$.201772$) and rare loss $.051586$ ($.008319$). Its rare-loss differences
against none, SD, and damping are $.045630$ ($.008323$), $.046822$ ($.008189$),
and $.050904$ ($.008322$), respectively. Each exceeds two paired MCSE. The
aggregate differences do not. Variance's aggregate median is $.069802$ and
maximum $6.505207$; rare selection raises its aggregate mean to $25.5720$ (MCSE
$8.0453$).

The two primary selections at factor $6.4$ have finite, worse validation scores
at the newly tested neighbor $12.8$. Across both Mushroom sizes, all arms, caps,
objectives, and the two expanded stopping searches, no positive-budget selection
uses either grid edge. At original stops, all selected predictors match the
nested 17-factor search. Sixty candidate indices change only because the same
initialization is assigned a lower factor under the deterministic tie rule.
Refined stops do select some added rates, up to $102.4$, and remain separate
comparisons.

\paragraph{Synthetic tails and unresolved cases.}

All eight laws and five arms are evaluated at equal effort. In the
centered/fitted rarer C2 law at cap $2048$ under aggregate selection, variance
scaling's exact rare-risk median is $.6836$, mean $1.727 \times 10^{30}$, MCSE
$1.552 \times 10^{30}$, and maximum $1.974 \times 10^{32}$. These tail-dominated
means, including those for SD and damping, are not precise expected-risk
estimates. Empty rare validation groups can still conceal severe prediction
error. For rare-only signal, variance's aggregate mean is $.01065$ ($.00311$),
but its rare mean is $.4122$ ($.1467$), versus $.2326$ ($.0319$) for none.

Across the two expanded stopping searches, the synthetic study retains 106
lower-edge and 52 upper-edge decisions, counting repeated queries on the same
replications. Upper selections occur only for variance scaling under known
preprocessing in the two rarer anchor laws. The Mushroom boundary conclusion
does not resolve these cases. The archives retain 6,451,200 endpoints and
387,072 choices across both stages, including 1,031,540 failed endpoints on
330,918 failed paths.

\paragraph{Independent audit and numerical limits.}

The audit reconstructs every loss and selection, verifies all 179 preserved
files, and replays 100,271 paths in explicitly transformed coordinates.
Overlapping frozen coefficient paths reproduce exactly. Reconstructed losses
have maximum normwise discrepancy $1.36 \times 10^{-14}$ and leave all
selections unchanged. Mushroom passes the declared trajectory and prediction
gates. Synthetic data retain 63 discrepancy records in twelve replications, all
with empty rare validation groups: 60 selected-prediction checks, two
first-failure times, and one trajectory check. Repeated queries can refer to the
same selected path. Maximum normwise coefficient and prediction discrepancies
are $.003747$ and $.004184$, exceeding the declared gates $2 \times 10^{-9}$ and
$10^{-8}$. All stored checkpoint statuses agree. These extreme synthetic paths
are not certified by the transformed replay; the original candidates and
selections remain archived. The result note records the affected configurations
and the extension of the checker to retain failure-time disagreements rather
than stopping at the first one.
